## Nat
### Journal
Journal is a project about journaling and writing about your day. The interface of this web page is simple and gives the user a clear direction to write their name and the enter a journal to describe their day. The project looks simple on the surface but hides something very interesting on the 'past entries' page. In this page, users get to explore the data related past entries. The data being displayed on this page consists of; the amount of time the previous user took typing, how long they were actively typing, the types of words used (nouns, verbs, adjectives), the words they hesitated to type, how long that hesitation was, etc.

Displaying the data without the actual entry is such a great decision in my opinion because it keeps the user's privacy while also offering people a way to view the writing process of the individual. I think this project is very clever because it made me realize we rarely ever take the time to appreciate the process of writing and I feel like this project brings light to that. It helps us notice the patterns we take when writing and maybe even relate to others who write in similar ways.

I think this project is very strong already and to take it even further I think maybe there could be a data compiler that displays the 'Hesitation of the Day.' This data would compile all the data entries of the day and rank which words most people hesitated to say. This could be a great way to see what people find hard to say and help users have a clear view of the writing patterns we share as humans. Since this data will be based on daily entries, each day may have a different word that people hesitated to say.

### Constellations of Strangers
Constellation of strangers is an interactive web page that allows users to anonymously share their thoughts and emotions. The project has a home page that displays three options: leave a star, explore the constellations and about. In the leave a star page, users are prompted to write what's on their mind and to select an emotion out of the list of emotions given to them. After submitting their information, they are led to the constellations page which displays each user's entry in the form of a star. Star colours represent the emotion the user selected and the stars linked by lines represent entries that have the same emotion. 

The constellation theme of this project was really well executed. Symbolizing connected emotions through constellations was a clever idea and the visuals do a great job at displaying that. Everything looks cohesive, from the colour palette to the visuals, and animation of the lines connnecting to each star to form a constellation.

The only recommendation I have for this project would be to keep in mind its scalability meaning to think about ways to display the stars in a way where don't overlap when there are too many entries. Two ideas came to mind as a solution for this. My first idea was to make the star size responsive to the amount of entries the site the receives. In that way, the more users there are, the smaller the stars become allowing more space on the page for new entries. However, the stars might become too small if there are thousands and thousands of entries. So my second idea would be to clear the entries from the day before and only show stars from the users who submitted their emotion on the same day.

### Cloud Reading
Cloud Reading is game that displays random cloud shapes on the screen and allows users to enter their guess of what the cloud looks like. By clicking on the cloud you are allowed to enter an object that resembles the cloud's appearance. 

I really love this project and I think it is a very unique concept. I like the cloud animation moving through the screen and also love the early 2000's windows aesthetic that was chosen. I find that the project also does a great job at making the game atmosphere look peaceful since cloud reading for me was a form of escapism when I was younger. I used it as a way to disassociate from every thing else and just let my mind wonder.

With this project I felt like displaying the cloud guess on the cloud was a good idea but I think it takes away from the player experience to fully let their mind wonder. By looking at the displayed guesses, the guesses of the player might unconciously be directed towards the guess they see on the screen, so I think it'll a good idea to only show the previous guesses of each cloud after the player has already inputed their cloud guess.

I had the idea that implementing a mechanic inspired by the game Krillion, would give players a goal when interacting with this project. The mechanic I thought of was to add a leaderboad that displays the most popular guesses for a certain cloud, and at the end of the day the cloud gets named after the most popular guess. I think this mechanic will form some type of community around the game where people rally up to enter the guesses and fight for a cloud to be named a certain way. I also think after each day the game should generate new randomized clouds to keep the community alive and wanting to come back for more.
